<Callout title="Methods for the model's datasets">
This page is the full write-up behind the [Datasets](/dry-lab/model/#datasets) subsection of the Model page. Every dataset described here can be downloaded from [huggingface.co/petadex](https://huggingface.co/petadex).
</Callout>

## Two datasets

The model learns from two datasets that we publish separately and join on sequence MD5. The PETadex corpus supplies the candidates the model scores, and the PETai activity labels supply the targets it learns to predict. Neither contains the other: the corpus carries no activity data, and the labels carry no corpus annotations. Protein language model embeddings are in neither.

## The corpus

The corpus holds 307,155,746 open reading frames (ORFs): 211 from PAZy, 4,723,128 from NCBI nr and 302,432,407 assembled from public sequencing runs through Logan. Each ORF comes with every annotation we could key to it: catalytic domain calls, signal peptides, biochemical properties, taxonomy, structures and the metadata of the sample it came from.

The corpus is highly redundant. Only 40% of the ORFs are distinct sequences, half of its 18,172,960 clusters at 90% identity are singletons, and the largest 6,591 clusters hold half of all ORFs. We therefore adapted the protein language model on the 90% cluster centroids rather than on raw ORFs, split 98 / 1 / 1 into training, validation and test sets.

## The activity labels

The labels hold 13,092 activity and stability measurements on 1,219 sequences. They come from 696 published papers, through the human and language-model extraction described on the Model page, and from our own halo screen.

| Source | Labels |
|---|---|
| Hand-curated activity | 6,655 |
| LLM-extracted | 4,898 |
| Published kinetics | 1,342 |
| Our halo screen | 197 |

The labels are not one quantity. Relative activity, melting temperature and the kinetic constants kcat, Km and kcat/Km are separate targets with different units, which is why Activity Prediction runs several label definitions in parallel. The cleanest are binary activity from the halo screen, for classification, and PET monomer release, for regression.

Confirmed inactive enzymes are rare. Only 200 labels are negative, 168 of them from our screen, and the LLM-extracted labels contain none, since papers seldom report enzymes that did not work. The halo screen measured BHET hydrolysis, so its negatives mean that no BHET hydrolysis was detected, not that the enzyme cannot degrade PET.

## Splitting

We split the labelled sequences at 30% identity so that close relatives never fall on both sides of the split, and checked the result for leakage. The 796 training sequences fall into only 70 clusters, so their effective diversity is closer to 70 enzyme families than to 796 sequences. Confidence intervals are therefore bootstrapped over clusters, not sequences.

The released split is homology-clean but unevenly stratified. Its test fold holds few negatives and a much larger share of LLM-extracted labels than the training fold, so it should be treated as a cluster assignment and re-stratified before it is used for evaluation. The dataset card describes this in detail.

## Distance to the candidates

The labelled set sits far from the population the model is meant to screen. Only 401 of the 1,219 labelled sequences appear in the corpus by exact sequence, and only 21 of those are Logan ORFs. Measurement conditions show the same gap: pH is recorded for about half of the labels and temperature for under a third, but neither exists for a sequence mined from Logan. A model that uses them in training has to work without them when it scores candidates.

## Downloading the data

| Dataset | What it holds | Role in the model |
|---|---|---|
| [petadex/petadex-corpus-centroids90](https://huggingface.co/datasets/petadex/petadex-corpus-centroids90) | One row per 90% identity cluster, with member summaries | Recommended starting point |
| [petadex/petadex-corpus-full](https://huggingface.co/datasets/petadex/petadex-corpus-full) | Every ORF in the corpus with every annotation layer | Candidates to score |
| [petadex/catalytic-orfs-90pid](https://huggingface.co/datasets/petadex/catalytic-orfs-90pid) | 90% identity centroid sequences, split for training | PlasticESM pretraining |
| [petadex/petai-activity-labels](https://huggingface.co/datasets/petadex/petai-activity-labels) | Measured activity and stability labels with a homology-aware split | PETai training labels |

Each dataset card documents its columns, how its tables join and its known caveats. Every table loads with the Hugging Face `datasets` library, and for joins across the full corpus, querying the Parquet files directly with DuckDB or Polars is much faster.

```python
from datasets import load_dataset

centroids = load_dataset("petadex/petadex-corpus-centroids90", split="train")
labels = load_dataset("petadex/petai-activity-labels", "labels", split="train")
```
